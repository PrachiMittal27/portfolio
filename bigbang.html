The final video documentation

The Brief

The brief was to create an interactive book around any chosen topic, using interaction, visual storytelling, and engaging page experiences to make the subject more than just something to read.

The Idea

I chose The Big Bang Theory to create a fun, interactive book for fans, moving from the show’s origins to its characters, relationships, and friendships. Each section turns a character trait or memorable moment into an interactive experience, supported by comic-inspired visuals, bold typography, and playful elements. The book also includes bonus sticker/reward pages and explores different printing techniques such as die-cutting, holographic foiling, collagraph, and screen printing to make the physical experience more engaging.

The Process

I began with layout ideation, sketching different page structures and interactive mechanisms based on each character and chapter. I then explored multiple cover directions and visual treatments before finalising the comic-inspired identity. The book went through draft printing and physical testing, allowing me to check scale, placement, folds, and interactions. Finally, I refined the layouts, produced the final prints and pasting, and assembled the interactive elements, including cut-outs, stickers, and layered components, into the finished book.

Visual Identity

The visual language was built directly from the world of the series. I used the show’s red, yellow, black, and cream palette, comic-book-inspired graphics, bold typography, speech bubbles, halftone textures, starbursts, and scientific diagrams. The visual style combines the scientific world of the characters with the playful, exaggerated energy of the sitcom, making the book feel like an extension of the show rather than a conventional editorial book. The cover also incorporates the show’s iconic atom motif and character imagery.

What I Wanted to Achieve

The project was about turning fandom into an experience. Rather than creating a book that simply tells the reader about The Big Bang Theory, I wanted the reader to play with the characters, interact with their personalities, revisit memorable moments, and feel like they were stepping into the show’s universe. The final result is an interactive fan book that combines editorial design, illustration, visual storytelling, games, puzzles, and collectible elements into one experience.
